Everything below was measured on the board. Where something is still open it says so
in the same sentence.

buzzer, microphone) and a HAL with `Board`, an I2C transport trait and two drivers.

## Fall detection, calibrated on nine real drops

Three rounds of drops by hand, recorded by the board: two at 200 Hz, five at 200 Hz, four
at 400 Hz. The classifier now detects **2/2, 3/3 and 4/4** with **no false alarm** on the
handling recording, and the four constants that matter were corrected by the data:

| what the first version assumed | what the drops showed |
|---|---|
| impact > 2000 mg means a fall | nine real landings were 1148–12823 mg; handling peaks at 1801 — the ranges overlap, so **free fall** is the discriminator, and 1100 mg is enough to call a landing |
| the impact follows the free fall immediately | it came 120 ms and 400 ms later; the detector found **zero of two** drops until a window was added |
| "still" is 700–1300 mg for 800 ms | the board bounces on its cable; "quiet" (<1500 mg) for 600 ms detects 4/4 where 800 ms detected 3/4 |
| 200 Hz is enough | two drops lost their free fall between samples; at 400 Hz the shortest is 42 ms ≈ 17 samples |

The image records while it classifies (10 s ring, 4000 samples), so the run that
demonstrates the detector is the run that produces the next dataset, and it sounds the
buzzer — three beeps, advanced by the sampling loop so classification never pauses.
Detail: `04-reference/FALL-DETECTION-CALIBRATION.md`.

## The five defects that explain the session

1. **The IMU was on a different bus, with the lines swapped.** Seeed's driver says
   `Wire1`; the Arduino variant claims SDA1=PB3/SCL1=PB2; the board has SDA=PB2,
   SCL=PB3. Nothing else was wrong — address, power gate and pull-ups were all right,
   which is why four earlier scans of "the right pins" found nothing.
   → `04-reference/IMU-INVESTIGATION.md`

2. **The microphone is analogue, and `IADC.SINGLE` is only writable while the
   converter is enabled.** Writing it disabled is a precise bus fault that leaves the
   engine in `EN.DISABLING`, a state that survives a core reset. Three attempts failed
   before the vendor comment settled it: *"IADCn->SINGLE has WSYNC type and can only be
   written while enabled"*.
   → `04-reference/IADC-MIC-NOTES.md`

3. **`format_fixed` takes value×100.** Passing milligrams raw printed `a=9,0,1` for a
   reading of 975 mg — indistinguishable from a dead accelerometer. The raw-byte dump
   in `hal_imu_regs` is what proved the sensor was right and the printout wrong.

4. **The linker gave 4.5 KB of RAM.** `_stack_start = 0x20001208` is the Arduino
   core's stack pointer, not a memory budget. A 6 KB capture buffer failed to link
   with "stack end address is not below stack start". Now the stack sits under the
   mailbox page and the linker has 252 KB.

5. **The buzzer calibration was wrong twice.** `CNT` wraps every 3.4 ms with
   `TOP=0xFFFF`, so a 10 ms window measures the remainder; and `asm::delay(n)` is not
   `n` cycles, which inflated the rate by exactly 3.0. It now counts CPU cycles with
   DWT.

## Open, and how to continue

1. **MVP does not execute.** `pac_mvp_add` builds and flashes; the engine accepts the
   writes but the result vector stays zero. Next step is not another guess: run a
   Silicon Labs sample as the oracle, halt before `CMD`, dump the register block and
   diff. → `04-reference/MVP-NOTES.md`
2. **A real fall dataset.** Three captures exist; all are handling, not falls. The
   classifier is honest about that (`selftest real=clean`), but a fall detector should
   be trained on drops.
3. **microSD, Grove UART/ADC, servo.** Not started. The microSD chip select shares a
   pin — the BSP facade is where that conflict should be refused.
4. **Old BSP modules** (`board.rs`, `pins.rs`, radio and sensor modules) still build
   but predate the current HAL API. They are not what the facade uses.
5. **Radio.** Last, as agreed.
